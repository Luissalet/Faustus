## When to Use

A student has an exam on a known date and a syllabus or notes, and wants to turn them into practice questions, a realistic daily plan and mock exams. This procedure covers building and running that loop. It does not teach the subject from nothing, and it does not guess exam content that the syllabus does not support.

## Procedure

1. Read the app's tool list. Collect the facts: exam date, format (multiple choice, short answer, oral, problems), duration, weight of each part, hours the student can really study per day, and a self-assessment of each topic (strong, shaky, unknown).
2. Build the subject as topics that mirror the syllabus headings, not your own taxonomy. If the student's own notes are the source, add them to the notebook so answers can cite them.
3. Create questions per topic in the exam's format. Include the key concepts and an explanation of why the right answer is right. Check a sample of each batch against the source before accepting the batch: wrong questions do more damage than missing ones. Mix recall, application and the typical traps.
4. Generate the day-by-day plan backwards from the exam date. Reserve the last day or two for light review, reserve catch-up days, schedule new material early, and make reviews arrive at growing intervals. Count the minutes: a plan that needs four hours a day from someone with two will fail on day three.
5. Run daily sessions with grading. For free-text or spoken answers, grade by meaning against the key concepts, and show the student what was missing, not only a mark.
6. After a week, read the weak-topic results. Shift time from topics already mastered to those with repeated errors, and ask for mock exams built on the weak topics under the real time limit.
7. Review mistakes by cause: did not know, knew but misread, calculation slip, ran out of time. Each cause has a different remedy; write one concrete action per cause.
8. In the final days, run one full mock in exam conditions and update the plan from its result. Report the readiness per topic honestly.

## Pitfalls

- A question bank copied wholesale from a source with unverified answers.
- Plans that ignore the days the student is not available.
- Treating a correct guess as knowledge; mark confidence as well as correctness.
- Spending study time on topics that feel productive because they are already known.
- Mock exams with no time limit, which hides pacing problems.
- Reading a high average as readiness when one heavily weighted topic is weak.

## Verification

- Every syllabus heading has at least one topic and questions with verified answers.
- The plan's total daily minutes fit the student's stated availability and end before the exam date with review.
- At least one mock exam was taken on the weak topics under a time limit.
- Each recurring mistake has a recorded cause and action.
