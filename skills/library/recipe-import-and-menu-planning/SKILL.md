## When to Use

The person has a recipe in a video, a web page or a block of text and wants it saved correctly, or wants a week of meals that uses what they already have. This procedure covers importing, reviewing, planning and the shopping list. It does not cover nutrition advice for medical conditions, and it does not order groceries.

## Procedure

1. Read the app's tool list. Import the recipe as a draft, never straight into the saved collection. A video import rebuilds the recipe from captions and description, so expect gaps.
2. Review the draft against the source line by line: ingredient names, quantities with units, number of servings, oven temperature, times and the order of the steps. Where the source gives no quantity ("a bit of oil"), leave it vague and flagged rather than inventing a number.
3. Fix the unit problems that matter: cups against grams, tablespoon against teaspoon, Celsius against Fahrenheit. Check servings last, because every scaled quantity depends on it.
4. Save the recipe only when the draft reads like something you could cook from without the original open.
5. Before planning, check the pantry: what is there, in which place, and what expires soon. Correct stale entries; a pantry that is wrong produces a wrong plan.
6. Plan the week. Start from the constraints: number of people, meals to cover, days away, dietary rules, the weekly budget. Place food that expires soonest early in the week, reuse a cooked base across two meals, and keep at least one low-effort day. Ask for the plan to be costed if prices are known.
7. Produce one consolidated shopping list from the menu: it should subtract the pantry stock, add the same ingredient across recipes into one line, and keep units compatible. Read the list against two recipes by hand to confirm the arithmetic.
8. Give the person the menu, the list, and the items you assumed they have.

## Pitfalls

- Trusting a video import as complete; the spoken quantities are often not in the captions.
- Counting a pantry item that is expired or already used up.
- Scaling a recipe by servings without scaling pan size and cooking time.
- Summing "1 onion" and "200 g onion" as if they were one unit.
- Planning seven different recipes when two cooked bases would do, which raises cost and effort.
- Forgetting that salt, oil and spices are in the pantry only if someone recorded them.

## Verification

- The saved recipe has servings, units and steps that match the source, and flagged gaps are listed.
- Every menu meal is linked to a saved recipe, and the items closest to expiry appear in the first days.
- The shopping list has no line for something the pantry already covers in full.
- The total cost or the budget comparison is stated when the plan was costed.
