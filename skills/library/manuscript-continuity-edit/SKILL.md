## When to Use

You are asked to revise, extend or cut a chapter or scene in a longer work of fiction, and the rest of the book already contains facts the new text must respect. This covers querying, editing and logging. It does not cover rewriting the author's voice wholesale or deciding plot direction; those belong to the author. The assistant bridge in the app is off by default and needs the author to switch it on.

## Procedure

1. Read the app's tool list and confirm the bridge is enabled. Read the target passage and the author's request. State in one line what the edit should achieve (tension, clarity, pace, a changed event) and what must not change.
2. Query before touching. Search the manuscript and the story notes for every fact the passage involves: names and spellings, ages, injuries, objects carried, places, who knows what, the weather and time of day, earlier mentions of the same event. Read the chapters just before and after the passage, not only the matching lines.
3. Build a continuity sheet for the passage: character state at the start (location, knowledge, wounds, mood), at the end, and the timeline position (day, hour, elapsed time since the previous scene). Mark each entry with where in the book it comes from.
4. Plan the change on paper. List which facts it alters, and where else in the manuscript those facts appear. A changed event usually needs changes in later chapters; decide whether you do them, or flag them to the author.
5. Edit with the author's voice as the guide: sentence length, point of view, tense and vocabulary of nearby text. Make the smallest change that achieves the aim. If a rewrite is requested, keep the beats the rest of the book depends on.
6. Re-check against the continuity sheet after editing: every fact still agrees with the earlier and later chapters, time passes plausibly, no character knows what they have not learned, no object appears without having been picked up.
7. Log what changed: the passage, the facts altered, the chapters that may now conflict and what you did about each. Keep the log where the author will see it, with the old text recoverable.
8. Report briefly: what was done, what you checked, and open questions for the author, with the evidence for each conflict you found.

## Pitfalls

- Editing from memory of the chapter instead of reading its neighbours.
- Fixing a continuity error in one place and leaving the original fact in three others.
- Changing what a character knows, which silently rewrites later scenes.
- Smoothing the prose into your style.
- Treating a typo in an earlier chapter as canon without asking.
- Large rewrites with no log, so the author cannot tell what moved.

## Verification

- Every fact in the edited passage was looked up, and conflicts are listed.
- Timeline and character states agree at both ends of the passage.
- The change log names affected chapters and how each was handled.
- The author's unresolved questions are written down, not decided silently.
