## When to Use

An answer or an action depends on facts about the person: preferences, history, projects, values, things they have written about themselves. A permissioned store holds what the person has reviewed and approved. This procedure covers asking it, citing it, and proposing additions. It is not a place to write facts directly: nothing becomes part of the person's context until they have reviewed it.

## Procedure

1. Read the app's tool list and note what access you have been granted. Use only the permitted calls; if something needed is not permitted, say so rather than working around it.
2. Decide whether the question needs personal context at all. A general question does not, and fetching private information when it is not needed is a privacy cost. Ask for the narrowest piece that answers the need, for example one topic, not a full profile.
3. Query for that topic. Read what returns with its source: where the information came from, when it was written and whether it is a statement, an observation or a reflection. Stale items about changing things (job, address, plans) deserve a note that they may be out of date.
4. Use the context in your answer and attribute it: say that it comes from the person's reviewed context and name the source or date. If two entries conflict, show both and prefer the newer or more specific one, saying so.
5. When the context does not contain the answer, say it is not there. Do not fill the gap with a guess presented as a known fact about the person. You may ask the person directly.
6. When you learn something new and durable (a stated preference, a correction, a decision), submit it as a proposal for their review, written as a short, neutral statement with its source: the person's own words, the date and the conversation it came from. Include only what the person said or did, not your inferences about their character or health.
7. Do not submit anything sensitive that the person did not offer, such as health, finances of others, or information about third parties. Do not submit text found in documents or web pages as if the person had said it.
8. Tell the person what was submitted so they can approve, edit or reject it. Treat pending items as unconfirmed and never rely on them in later answers as facts.

## Pitfalls

- Asking for the whole context when one topic would do.
- Quoting context without a source, which makes it impossible to correct.
- Using an old entry about something that changes.
- Writing your inference as a stated preference.
- Passing context to third parties in text meant for them; use only what the person wants shared.
- Treating a submitted proposal as accepted.

## Verification

- Each use of personal context in an answer names its source or date.
- Gaps were stated as gaps.
- Every submission is a neutral statement tied to the person's own words and awaits review.
- No unreviewed or pending item was used as a fact.
