## When to Use

The person wants to understand how their projects, product catalogue, posts and sales are performing, to explain a change, or to keep track of companies and opportunities. Figures come from outside services when refreshed, and history is stored locally. This procedure does not forecast revenue or guarantee that a cause is the cause; it shows evidence for a cause and its strength.

## Procedure

1. Read the app's tool list. Refresh the figures you need and note the time of the refresh and the source of each. If a source failed to refresh, say so; do not present stored old numbers as current.
2. Fix the question: which metric, which period, compared with what. Choose comparison periods of equal length and, where it matters, equal composition: same number of weekdays, same weekends, similar season. Comparing a month with a holiday to one without produces a drop that is not a trend.
3. Look at the shape before the totals. Check whether a change is a step (one day), a ramp or a one-off spike, and whether it comes from one product, post or channel. Break totals by product, channel and country; a total can stay flat while one item rose and another fell.
4. Normalise when volumes differ: per day, per visitor, per published post. Report both the absolute and the relative change, and the base: "up 50 percent from 2 to 3 sales" is not news.
5. Tie a change to a cause with a timeline. List events near the change: a publication, a price change, a new product, a platform change, an outage. A cause must come before the effect, match the part of the numbers that moved, and not be undone by the other explanations. State the result as supported, plausible or unknown.
6. Be careful with imported files. Spreadsheets from publishing platforms may use presets that were not checked against real exports; read the first rows and compare totals with the platform's own screen before trusting an import.
7. Keep the CRM current: for each company record the contact, what they need, the stage of the opportunity, the next step with a date, and the date of last contact. Update the stage when something happens, not in batches, and close opportunities with a reason.
8. Report with the figures, the periods compared, the likely cause with its strength, and the one next action per opportunity that is due.

## Pitfalls

- Comparing unequal periods.
- Reading a total without breaking it down.
- Declaring a cause from one coincidence in time.
- Mixing currencies or tax-inclusive and tax-exclusive prices.
- Trusting an import preset without comparing totals.
- Opportunities that stay open with no next step.

## Verification

- Each figure has a source and a refresh time.
- Compared periods have the same length, and the report says if they differ in composition.
- A claimed cause comes with the dated event and a strength label.
- Import totals match the source platform's own totals.
- Every open opportunity has a next step with a date.
