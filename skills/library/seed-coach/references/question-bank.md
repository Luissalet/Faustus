# Investor question bank (seed-coach)

Format: question, what the investor is testing, what a strong answer has. Tailored questions use your own numbers: ask one only when every number it needs is in `seed/` (competitors, model, market, investors, plan); otherwise skip it, never fill it with a guess. Each round: one tailored question per category first, then generic ones.

## Market
- Tailored: "Your SOM is {som_share} of a {sam} SAM in {som_years} years. What gets you the first 1,000 customers?" Tests: does the market slide connect to a real go-to-market. Strong: one channel, its cost per customer, how many customers it already produced.
- Tailored: "Your market size rests on an assumption ({assumption}). Where does that number come from?" Tests: whether you know which numbers are guesses. Strong: the source, or an honest "it is an assumption" and how you will test it.
- Who exactly is the customer, and how many are there? Tests: focus. Strong: one customer, one number, one source.
- Why is this a venture-scale market and not a nice small business? Strong: the path from the first city to a large yearly revenue, with numbers.
- What happens to demand in a recession? Strong: how your customer behaved in the last downturn, with a number.

## Competition
- Tailored: "{rival} has {ratings} ratings. Why will {customers} switch?" Tests: is your edge big enough to beat habit. Strong: the pain with evidence ("N of M recent low reviews mention it") and the moment someone switches.
- Tailored: "{rival}'s reviews are about {weak_point}. What stops it fixing that next quarter?" Tests: defensibility. Strong: why the incumbent's business model makes the fix costly for them.
- Tailored: "{second_rival} users complain about {weak_point}. How do you make sure that never happens to you?" Tests: operations, not slogans. Strong: the mechanism, what it costs, what happens when it fails.
- Who else is building this right now? Strong: names, what they do differently, why you win anyway.
- What is your unfair advantage? Strong: one specific hard-to-copy thing (a channel, data, a cost, a team fact).
- Why hasn't the market leader already done this? Strong: the cost or conflict that stops them, with a number.

## Traction
- What have you proven so far? Strong: users, revenue, retention, a waitlist or pilot, with dates.
- How fast are you growing, week over week? Strong: one growth rate over a stated period.
- What does a customer who stays look like, and why do they stay? Strong: a cohort number and the reason in the customer's words.
- What did you learn that surprised you? Strong: a specific finding and what you changed.

## Unit economics
- Tailored: "What does your {take_rate} take rate leave after {provider_pay}?" Tests: does the business make money per order. Strong: revenue and contribution per order in money. Have ready: price, what you keep, contribution.
- Tailored: "Your LTV:CAC is {ltv_cac}. What CAC did you assume, and what have you actually paid?" Strong: the assumed CAC, the real CAC so far, the channel. Have ready: CAC, LTV, payback months.
- Tailored: "You break even in month {breakeven_month}. What has to be true for that?" Strong: two or three drivers and how sensitive breakeven is to each. Have ready: peak burn, year 1 and year 3 revenue.
- What is your gross margin, and how does it change at scale? Strong: the margin today and the one cost that falls with scale.
- How do you set your price, and what happens if you raise it 20%? Strong: how you tested price and what customers did.
- What does it cost you to get one customer today, and through which channel? Strong: a figure from real spend, the channel, payback in months.

## Team
- Why are you the team to build this? Strong: a specific past result that maps to this problem.
- Who is your next key hire, and why? Strong: the gap, the role, when it starts.
- How do the founders split decisions, and what happens when you disagree? Strong: a real example.
- Are you full time, and how much runway do you personally have? Strong: a straight yes and a date.

## Why now
- Why couldn't this have been built five years ago? Strong: one change in the world, with a date or number.
- What changes in the next two years that makes this bigger? Strong: a trend with a source.
- What would make this a bad time to start? Strong: the headwind, named, and why you start anyway.

## Risks
- Tailored: "The model shows a peak burn of {peak_burn} a month. What do you cut if the raise takes six months longer?" Strong: the cut list in order and the runway it buys. Have ready: runway months.
- What is the single biggest risk to this company? Strong: one risk, named plainly, and the test that retires it.
- What has to go right that you do not control? Strong: the dependency and plan B.
- What regulation, liability or insurance issue could hurt you? Strong: the specific rule or exposure and how you handle it.

## The ask
- Tailored: "You are raising {raise}. Walk me through where it goes and what it gets you." Strong: the use of funds and the milestone it reaches before the next round. Have ready: the biggest line and its amount.
- Tailored: "{investor_first_name}'s record: {last_deal}. How is {startup} different, and do you want {firm} to lead?" Tests: homework on this investor. Strong: what you know about their deal, the difference in one line, a clear yes on the role you want. Have ready: their usual check size.
- Who else is in the round, and who is leading? Strong: names or the honest state, and the close date.
- What valuation or cap are you raising at, and why? Strong: the number and the comparable rounds behind it.
- What milestones will you hit before you raise again? Strong: two or three numbers with dates.
