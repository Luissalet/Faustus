## When to Use

The person meets or talks to people and wants a trustworthy memory: who is who, what was said, what was promised and when to get back in touch. It covers recording, follow-ups and meeting preparation. It does not cover mass mailing, and it should not hold sensitive details that the person would not want written down.

## Procedure

1. Read the app's tool list. Before creating anyone, search by name and by aliases. Names are ambiguous; when the search returns several candidates, ask which one instead of choosing. Creating a duplicate splits the history.
2. Record the person: how you know them, where they work or live in broad terms, circles (family, clients, friends), and how they like to be addressed. Add other names and nicknames as aliases so later mentions resolve.
3. Store facts as short statements with a date, one idea each ("moved to Lisbon in March", "allergic to nuts", "daughter starts school in September"). Keep to what the person said or you observed. Leave out guesses about motives or health unless the person said them and wants them kept.
4. Log each conversation as an interaction: date, channel, what was discussed, what changed. Capture the commitments in both directions: who promised what, by when. These are the most valuable entries and the easiest to lose.
5. Decide the follow-up at the time of logging. Ask what outcome you are waiting for, then set a reminder with a date and a specific first sentence ("ask whether the contract arrived"), not "get in touch". Choose the date by the other person's timetable, not only yours.
6. Review proposed commitments that come from meeting minutes before accepting them; they are suggestions that need a decision, and a wrongly attributed promise damages trust.
7. Prepare for a meeting by pulling the brief: last contacts, open commitments, recent facts, upcoming dates. Read it, pick two things to ask and one thing to deliver, and note anything sensitive to avoid.
8. After the meeting update the record the same day while it is fresh, close fulfilled commitments and move reminders.

## Pitfalls

- Duplicate records because an alias was not checked.
- Writing opinions of a person as if they were facts.
- Reminders without an action, which get ignored.
- Marking a commitment done on your side only when it depends on them.
- Putting the CRM to use for a person who has asked not to be tracked.
- Contacting someone on a birthday reminder with a generic message that shows it was a reminder.

## Verification

- Each person appears once and has aliases for the names used in conversation.
- Every logged interaction has a date and any commitment has an owner and due date.
- Every open follow-up has a reminder with a specific action.
- The meeting brief matches the stored history and was read before use.
